Welcome! I am a Research Fellow at the Saïd Business School, University of Oxford, and a Research Assistant at the University of Chicago's Knowledge Lab. I hold an M.A. in International Relations from the University of Chicago, where I also received my B.A. in Political Science.

My research focuses on topics in political economy, development, and comparative politics. My current work uses applied econometrics and machine learning to investigate how economic and technological change shape political institutions.

Working Papers

Innovation without Democratization: The Political Limits of Technological Change
M.A. thesis, University of Chicago  ·  Advisor: Scott Gehlbach  ·  PDF

Abstract. Innovation has been positively linked to a variety of economic outcomes, including long-run development, productivity, and human capital. But its political effects have received far less attention. To address this imbalance, I investigate the effect of innovation on democracy and two intermediate outcomes, state capacity and capital mobility, using a shift-share instrumental variables strategy on a panel of 187 countries from 1995 to 2015. This design exploits quasi-random variation in global patenting growth across different fields of knowledge to identify the effects of innovation. I find that innovation exerts no detectable effect on democracy, but strongly and positively increases both state capacity and capital mobility. A one-standard-deviation increase in log patents per million residents raises state capacity by approximately half a standard deviation and capital mobility by roughly a quarter of a standard deviation. I argue that the null effect of innovation on democracy stems from the offsetting influences of these two forces. State capacity reduces the elites' costs of repression and therefore inhibits democratization, while capital mobility decreases the costs of conceding to democracy and thus promotes it. Consistent with this account, I find that innovation widens the gap between the state's capacity and society's ability to hold it accountable. A widening gap should erode democracy on its own, so paired with the null it suggests that capital mobility offsets the state's growing power. My estimates are robust across several specifications, measures, and samples.

Does Economic Globalization Promote Democracy? An Empirical Investigation into the Political Consequences of Economic Integration
Berkeley Economic Review, forthcoming 2026  ·  B.A. thesis  ·  Advisor: Susan Stokes  ·  PDF
Show abstract

Global economic integration has been a defining feature of the 21st century, as has a steady cross-national wave of democratic backsliding. Are these two trends related? Many studies have tackled the impact of economic globalization on democracy, but most do not model the persistence of democracy rigorously. I provide evidence that globalization's relationship to democracy varies depending on the aspect of democracy under study. Trade openness was found to have a negative association with electoral freeness and fairness, while foreign direct investment outflows were positively associated with equality before the law and civil liberties. The latter finding is heterogeneous across levels of development, with subsample results suggesting that the relationship is almost entirely driven by developing countries. I construct a panel of 143 countries from 1991 to 2023 and employ a dynamic panel model to estimate the relationship between globalization and democracy. Unlike other studies, I use a three-pronged operationalization of democracy, separately examining the statistical associations of globalization with electoral quality, civil liberties, and political equality. This granularization captures dynamics in globalization's link to democracy that may otherwise go unnoticed or be misattributed to democracy as a whole. The robustness of these results is examined under several different model specifications, estimation techniques, and sample modifications. Last, I propose a series of mechanisms that offer theoretical underpinnings to the empirical findings.
